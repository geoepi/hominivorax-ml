# Task 2D — Observation regime

The response is a record of human-reported insect detections rather than
standardized systematic surveillance. Therefore a zero in a node-week means
**no recorded detection**, not confirmed insect absence. The audit does not
construct a surveillance-effort denominator or infer observer effort,
inspection counts, detection probability, or biological absence.

## What is audited

The weekly audit spans `2024-W01` through `2026-W29` and reports:

- raw observation records, canonical positive cells, unique spatial locations,
  revised-domain cells, latitude and longitude extent, and north/southmost
  detections;
- reporting continuity using weeks with at least 1, 5, and 10 detections and
  weeks since the previous recorded detection;
- Mexico, U.S.-to-40°N, and excluded Central America south of Mexico summaries;
- positive node-week density and occupied-node coverage for each early-2025
  candidate start;
- latitude-band progression (`<15°N`, `15–20°N`, `20–25°N`, `25–30°N`,
  `30–35°N`, `35–40°N`); and
- seasonal coverage by ISO week-of-year, month, quarter, and fixed calendar
  season (winter = December–February, spring = March–May, summer = June–
  August, fall = September–November).

The Mexico/U.S. summaries describe recorded-detection density and continuity;
they are not surveillance-effort estimates. The U.S. domain remains in the
prospective prediction space even when its observed detection count is small.

## Domain provenance

Central America south of Mexico is quantified before removal. Its cells are
removed only because the fixed geographic rule retains Mexico and the existing
U.S. footprint below 40°N. The rule is independent of observed outcomes. The
canonical environmental-support intersection remains authoritative, and
livestock missingness cannot create or remove domain cells.

## Early-2025 transition

The candidate-start comparison is descriptive. `2025-W01` is preferred when
the early-2025 sequence is already sustained enough to support a complete
annual seasonal cycle; a later candidate is allowed only when early weeks are
clearly discontinuous under the documented reporting screen. More detections
later in the period alone are not a reason to discard the beginning of 2025.

In the authoritative audit, the 2024 interval contained 35,895 valid source
records but only two revised-domain positive weeks. The 2025-W01–2026-W29
interval contained 91,162 valid source records and all 81 revised-domain weeks
had at least one recorded detection. Revised-domain weekly detections rose from
5 in 2025-W01 to 300 in 2025-W13. These are recorded-detection summaries, not
estimates of surveillance effort.

The revised-domain observations were all in Mexico in this source window:
44,587 Mexico records were retained across 3,124 Mexico nodes, while the
6,913-node U.S. portion had no recorded detections. Observed detections reached
29.24°N, whereas the fixed canonical grid remains available through 40°N for
prospective prediction. The removed Central America region contained 78,251
observations assigned to removed canonical cells and had recorded detections in
all 133 audit weeks.

## Limitations

The audit can quantify recorded-detection continuity and information density,
but it cannot identify the surveillance process that generated those records.
Changes in reporting density, reporting geography, observer behavior, or data
pipeline coverage may change apparent occurrence patterns. Domain and time
restriction reduce obvious heterogeneity but do not correct reporting bias
statistically.
